# CGS Wiki bounded series comparison

## Boundary and evidence

Start with **Solo Leveling, season 1, episodes 1–2 inclusive**. End at the closing scene of episode 2. This covers the first raid and the discovery of the inner temple. The limit is what the audience can establish through that episode. It includes earlier history revealed within the two episodes, independent of when it occurs in-world. It excludes later explanations and outcomes, even if a later source assigns them to an earlier in-world date. Record the exact cut and source editions before authoring the final dataset. [Episode 1](https://www.crunchyroll.com/watch/GQJUMN470/im-used-to-it) · [Episode 2](https://www.crunchyroll.com/watch/GE00320670ARSA/if-i-had-one-more-chance)

Use the anime for what the anime edition discloses. Cite equivalent portions of the novel or comic by edition and chapter or page after comparison. For adaptations that differ, preserve the attribution and record compatibility, disagreement, or a separate edition. Full publication descriptions and volume numbers alone cannot establish the story cutoff. Fandom supplies a discovery list and an encyclopedia benchmark. Its current topic pages can combine later material with first-arc facts and require a fact-by-fact cutoff review. A public search index exposed the arc and temple page material when direct access was restricted. Record direct reference observations separately when available. [Authorized novel edition](https://yenpress.com/titles/9781975319274-solo-leveling-vol-1-novel) · [Authorized comic edition](https://yenpress.com/titles/9781975319434-solo-leveling-vol-1-comic) · [Fandom arc article](https://solo-leveling.fandom.com/wiki/D-Rank_Dungeon_Arc) · [Fandom temple article](https://solo-leveling.fandom.com/wiki/Cartenon_Temple)

Write original summaries and use independently authored illustrations or assets with permissions where needed. Use source links and specific locators. Keep the CGS design and world-model semantics. Compare topic coverage, article organization, fact boxes, related links, history, citation quality and how quickly the reader can find the answer to the same question.

## How to run and score the comparison

1. In a dedicated CGS world, record the source inventory and mark each entry as present with evidence, intentionally unknown, disputed by edition, outside the cutoff, or missing. Compare the same scoped article families in Fandom. A Fandom omission does not remove a verified primary-source fact. Give every missing or hard-to-find item its own reproducible report.
2. Starting at the world home, open the arc and then an individual, a location, an event and its source through normal links and search. Compare each answer, article hierarchy, lead, key facts, section findability, related pages, dates and citation trail on desktop and narrow screens. Record reference revision or access date, task steps and concrete discrepancies. Verify a representative save, its service response, PostgreSQL readback and browser reload for each data family involved.
3. Use author, reader and applicable character views. Test both omitted future records and withheld facts within the boundary, including through a direct link, search preview, related page, event, media and export when available. Use synthetic spoiler sentinels for access tests so a real later plot reveal need not be written into this dataset.
4. W5 passes when every included fact and link can be represented and found, the completed pages provide comparable density and source clarity for the bounded topics, and no tested path leaks later or protected material. An unresolved omission or capability gap keeps the affected check open. Run the separate operational-world walkthrough for cross-domain reuse.

## W1 pilot and open checks

The browser pilot creates a new world, two episode Source articles with distinct locators, one Character, one registered rank, one Place, and one occurrence with an explicitly unknown in-world date. It links the Character, Place, and episode Source to the occurrence, cites the other episode for the Character's registered rank and reopens the saved records. The pilot uses the same Wiki forms and read path as any other world.
