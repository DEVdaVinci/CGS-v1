# CGS Wiki field trace

Source **Wiki.pdf**, pages 1–4, from the Lucidchart export supplied by the project author. Page 1 describes the Wiki and timeline. Page 2 names the fields and illustrates places, things, and editorial records. Page 3 contributes no named fields. Page 4 shows the franchise, content, scene, shot, element, and impact graph. The related CGS diagrams and the author's feedback clarify the meaning of time, actor knowledge, and reusable rules. The [W1 plan](CGS_W1_Core_Worldbuilding.md) records which parts of the separate roadmap chat were available for review.

The **Prior build** column refers to the W1 implementation at `fc9e0e3`. **Omitted** means that the browser could not record the source concept in its own right. For fields added in this pass, the last column also says what made the prior omission consequential. “Later” identifies an explicit dependency and the acceptance test the eventual implementation needs. A field that is explainable through prose alone remains an omission when the source gives it a separate typed purpose.

| **Length**, p2 | Omitted | Added **Length (cm)** with the same precision and applicability as width. It supplies the third physical dimension independently of vertical height and width. |
| **Weight**, p2 | Mass on actors, definitions, and places | Extended **Mass (kg)** to items. Kilograms represent the object's authored mass. When a world's forces depend on local gravity, the physical calculation requires a gravity rule and a force unit and belongs with W2 rules and P2 execution. |
| **Personality**, p2 | Present | **Personality trait** is a qualified value on the organism or reusable definition. Core traits, current emotion, and an actor's beliefs have separate records because their time and meaning differ. Population personality is addressed below. |
| **Species**, p2 | Present | A typed **Species** link points to a reusable species profile. Characters and organisms can keep the same identity while a dated classification or explicit individual trait changes. |
| **Birth Day**, p2 | Present as Birth time | **Birth time** stores the fictional calendar coordinate. Its stable ordering supports historical reads and age without using the database creation date. Calendar formatting for months, custom eras, or finer clocks has its own discovery and calendar work. |
| **Birthplace**, current author decision | Omitted | The biography links the place where the character was born. **Place of origin** can name a different place they are from. Physical whereabouts is a separate dated relationship. |
| **Death date**, p2 | Present as Death time | **Death time** is independently nullable and uses the same fictional clock. Unknown stays visible as unknown. |
| **Date of creation**, p2 | Omitted | Added **Creation time** for items, places, groups, cultures, territories, assets, and species. An item can date back hundreds of fictional years regardless of when its author added it to the Wiki. An Era uses its period start and a role or trait definition can link to the event that introduces it. |
| **Date of destruction/extinction**, p2 | Omitted | Added **Destruction time** for nonliving subjects and **Extinction time** for species. They differ in meaning and both use the fictional calendar. They preserve the article and its history. Life-cycle actions and effects are authored through W2 and executed through P2. |

An era can span several event trees and can overlap another era. Its links are authored and support a timeline filter. Calendrical inference and richer date presentation remain separate work. |

| **Where**, pp1–2 | Present | **Places** are typed links. A group or cross-site event can have more than one. A participant's whereabouts remains independently dated canon. |
| **When**, pp1–2 | Present | Dated leaves have a world time and can have an explicit duration. A group can derive its dates from descendants or use an authored range. The prior consistency discrepancy between article slices and the world outline is still an open W1 check. |

W2 owns deeper temporal and story-rule checks. |

## Editorial and franchise graph

The named editorial fields and types below are deliberate pending work. Their time axis includes sequence, presentation, and reveal to an audience. One canonical event can be narrated by different episodes in different orders, or shown to only some audiences. Using core actor or event columns for these records would assign presentation decisions to the fictional world and would make the same event hard to reuse. When S1 introduces an approved outline and scene, it must link to existing event IDs and distinguish canonical order from scene order. The broader franchise graph has independently planned work beyond S1.

| Lucidchart field or node | Disposition and individual reason |
| --- | --- |
| **Editorial Name**, p2 | Belongs to the work or production unit. Its title may differ from the canonical event or location name. |
| **Summary / Synopsis**, pp1–2 | Belongs to the selected cut, chapter, or episode. It may convey only what that unit tells an audience. |
| **Outline / Overview**, pp1–2 | A planned order of scenes or beats can change without retiming events. S1 consumes canon through these references. |
| **Purpose**, pp1–2 | The author's narrative intention can differ from every character's goal. Event Purpose goals remain available now. |
| **Highlights / main events**, p2 | Editorial selection and prominence can put the same canonical event in multiple works. Source event links support this later. |
| **Key elements of intrigue**, including themes, patterns, jokes or bits, pp1–2 | These are annotated audience-facing devices that can cross episodes and scenes. They can refer to an event without changing its canonical fields. |
| **Theme / thematic elements**, pp1–2 | An interpretation is tied to a work or outline. Character values and motives remain authored independently in the Wiki. |
| **Vibe / mood**, pp1–2 | A scene can convey dread while a character feels relief. Editorial mood needs its own record when the scene is authored. |
| **Setting / summary of worldbuilding**, p2 | A work can choose a subset of places and lore. The Wiki remains the source of those linked records. |
| **Events** and **Timeline?**, pp1–2 | A work associates multiple canonical events with a disclosed order. Event IDs exist now. Disclosure and editorial order need the work context. |
| **Series; season, volume, book, movie, album; episode, chapter, act, song**, pp1–2 | Production units have different nesting and medium rules. Model them in the editorial catalog once the series and content contracts are defined. |
| **Scene; script; light novel**, pp1–2 | S1 can read event IDs into a scene with its own audience and point of view. Script and novel outputs are medium-specific consumers of the editorial content. |
| **Franchise → Content → Scene → Shot → Element → Impact**, p4 | Broader production data tracks the intended audience impact of a structural unit or element. A shot or element can have a production impact independently of a character's action, reaction, or state change. |

Blank **Field Type** and ellipsis rows in the diagrams are placeholders. They do not name properties. CGS records later proposals against a concrete field name and a reason.

## CGS fields and constraints added beyond the named source fields

Each row names the extra choice, including the prior W1 fields the author saw in the browser. Field keys, allowed kinds, type, multiplicity, and qualifiers are defined in the [Wiki field catalog](../backend/cgs/worlds/wiki_schema.py). [Wiki validation](../backend/cgs/worlds/wiki.py) and the [write handlers](../backend/cgs/worlds/wiki_writes.py) apply them for normal browser and API saves.

| CGS field or constraint | Reason for the addition or change |
| --- | --- |
| **ID, World, Kind** | Give references stable endpoints, prevent cross-world links, and let the browser select type-appropriate controls. A typed article represents a person, place, or item even when created via **New entry**. |
| **Description / Overview** | Phrases an author does not need to query still belong in the article. This also lets an author start with prose and add typed details when known. |
| **Author only** on an article or fact | An author can keep unrevealed canon. Viewpoint views use the actor's observations and beliefs. An access marker alone cannot imply the actor learned a fact. |
| **Effective from, Effective until, Source, Qualifier** on a fact | Provide when a value applies, where it came from, and the dimension of a repeated field. For example, Focus and Currency have separate amounts and sources. Values end just before their exclusive end tick. |
| **Location** on a person, organism, or item | Records current physical whereabouts at the selected fictional time. A dated move can change this value. A house someone owns, a destination they want to reach, or a rumor about where they were remains a separate record. |
| **Part of / Parent place** | Reuses the stored location endpoint for the Place kind and presents the structural meaning explicitly. A floor can be part of a building while the room's occupants change. |
| **Legacy location** on a group | Preserves ambiguous existing data for author review. New authors can use Group place for sites or an event link for where an organized group participated. |
| **Place type** | Lets Buildings, Wings, Floors, Rooms, and Terrain share spatial rules and measured layouts. Types define authoring vocabulary. |
| **Group type** and **Item type** | Wearers, owners, materials, tools, offices, and institutions share supported relationship mechanics. A browser category distinguishes source-named subtypes while keeping those mechanics and IDs reusable. |
| **Appearance** | Prose describes visible features the author has not turned into measurements. Image, dimensions and condition keep their specific forms. The text can be an authored definition default. |
| **Background** | Stores undated biography or context. **Backstory of** now provides the separate timeline links requested by the source. |
| **Class**, **Additional class**, and **Parent definition** | The roadmap design uses reusable classes and inheritance. One primary class retains prior semantics, several additional classes can contribute defaults, and definitions inherit within the same kind. An explicit individual trait wins over a default. Incompatible defaults require resolution. A class is a linked article with its own identity. |
| **Place of origin**, **Nationality or citizenship**, **Residence**, and **Destination** | Birthplace or origin, legal or social affiliation, residence, and intended destination can all point at different places and change under different conditions. One location field cannot answer each question. |
| **Physical trait** and **Body part** | Named traits and anatomies let an author add fields such as skin type, wing shape or ankle without adding a database column per anatomy. They can come from a reusable definition. |
| **Physical condition** and **Physical function** | An entity may keep the same body part and change its health or capabilities. A sprain describes the condition. Limited support describes its function until healed. Both can be dated with the same event. |
| **Parent**, **Sibling**, **Other family relationship**, **Personal relationship**, **Membership** and **Primary affiliation** | These specialize the source's relationships by direction, endpoints, or cardinality. Parent checks for cycles in overlapping time ranges. Siblings can be recorded explicitly or derived from a shared recorded parent. Membership allows several groups while primary affiliation serves a single authored classification. |
| **Assigned role**, **Job**, and **Title** | Authority or assignment, work performed, and an honorific can each begin or end separately. They share a Role or title definition with a group qualifier. Eligibility, grants, and ongoing duties require W2 rules. |
| **Owner**, **Possessor**, **Inventory quantity**, and **Related item** | A borrowed lamp can have different owners and holders. A pile of ten common lamps can use a quantity. Related item covers relationships with a role that conveys neither title nor custody. |
| **Contained in item** and **Material or component** | Contents can move out of a bag without changing the bag's constituent material. Date and link each to its own Item identity. Container cycles are invalid during overlapping intervals. |
| **Resource amount** and **Unit** | Needed for stamina, money, magical resources and institution balances without hard-coding one genre. The amount is exact and qualified by the linked Resource. Reusable quantitative Traits also accept a unit. Unit consistency across historical corrections remains an open check. |
| **Actual rating** and **Registered rating** | Actual ability and what an institution certifies can differ and have different sources. This represents the user's example while keeping both fields usable for other worlds. |
| **Value**, **Preference**, **Personality trait**, **Emotional state**, and **Mental state** | Give goals and the later cognitive system inputs with different stability and semantics. A preference with an indefinite date can remain through a short interval of fear or fatigue. |
| **Goal owner**, **Parent goal**, **Related subject**, **Priority**, and **Desired state** | A goal can outlast one action, be decomposed into subgoals, refer to a subject or a typed target fact, and be prioritized independently. Event Purpose goals can refer to it even when no effect fulfills it. |
| **Speaker**, **Statement time**, **Observer**, **Learned at**, **About time**, **Evidence**, and **Belief holder** | The author explicitly asked for the difference between canon and what a character knows or thinks. A perpetrator's account can be inaccurate, be heard later, and concern an earlier date. The receiver can form a different belief about the same record. |
| **Learned ability**, **Learned at**, **Ability used**, and **Used by** | Define a person's dated acquisition and an event's performance separately from the Ability identity. A typical species ability does not silently grant it to all individuals. |
| **Asset location** | Resolves a typed visual or map reference to an authored locator such as an asset path. It retains the storage location for future media integration. A browser asset viewer, uploading, 3D rendering, or layered map editing needs its own media contract. |
| **Calendar settings / Days per year** | Turns the numeric timeline into consistent age calculations without assuming that a year has Earth's length. The current scaffold uses one unit of world time per day for that calculation. |
| **Event group**, **Parent event**, **Position**, **Earliest / Latest time**, and **Related actions and occurrences** | The author's timeline hierarchy needs explicit composition and order, while a single historical action may be relevant in more than one group. Related actions add references without duplicating the action or its effects. |
| **State change mode** Set, Add, End | An event can replace an exclusive value, add a member to a repeatable relationship, or end a timed fact. The separate operations keep prior history and make effect semantics inspectable. |
| **Layout name, Left, Top, Width, Height, Waypoint name, X, Y, Connection endpoints, Direction, Length, Description, Source and effective dates** | The upcoming dungeon and other routes need measured coordinates and connected endpoints. Layout width and height measure the drawn plane. The physical width, length and height facts describe the subject. A route can have a measured path length different from the drawn straight line. |
| **Event cause, goal, era, backstory and effect references** | These realize the source nuances through existing record IDs. A correction to the event or linked article is reflected consistently wherever it is referenced. Causality and era membership are presently authored links. |
| **Consistency finding code, affected record IDs and interval** | An author can locate saved contradictions and changes in evidence in the affected article. W2 extends the checker to author-defined rules, dependencies, severity, and save policy. |
| **Edit counter, idempotency key, receipt, created timestamp** | Prevent concurrent overwrites or repeated submissions. These are application bookkeeping. World dates, corrections and optional future author revision history remain separate. |

## Checking the result in the browser

- **Wiki → New entry → Character or Organism** offers biography and measured traits, including birthplace, origin, nationality, and family. **Location history** on a saved article opens previously authored facts. Use **Record fact** for independently known whereabouts and **Record dated change** for a move. Image, 3D model, Texture, Animation and Map link to articles made under **New entry**.
- **Wiki → Library** opens Species, Era, Territory, Group and asset articles. Open a Species and use **Record fact → Physical measurement distribution** or **Power rating distribution** to add outcome and weight rows under a named Trait. The article shows the saved values and the relevant unit.
- **Wiki → Timeline → New event** or **New group** exposes **What happened**, **Summary**, **How**, and **Cause, purpose, and context**. That last control links to saved cause events, a specific dated fact under Cause state, Purpose goals, Backstory subjects, and Eras. The saved event appears on linked articles and follows the same ID in the outline.

The [Django field and event tests](../backend/cgs/worlds/test_wiki.py) check valid data, references, dates, cycles, and atomic rejection. The [browser tests](../frontend/e2e/wiki.spec.ts) author fields and follow event links through save, edit, and reload. The [W1 revision sequence](CGS_W1_Core_Worldbuilding.md#w1-revision-sequence) tracks the remaining scope and acceptance evidence.
